My name is Manmeet Singh, a Computer Science student at Sheridan College driven by a lifelong obsession with creating, game engineering, machine learning, and AI. Games have been part of my life since I was a kid, and what started as a love of playing them slowly became a need to understand them, and eventually, to build them.

I got into coding because I love solving problems. There's something deeply satisfying about facing something broken or unknown and finding the elegant path through it. That same instinct is what pulls me toward game development, because a great game is just a beautifully solved problem that millions of people get to experience.

To me, games are one of the highest form of creative engineering, sitting at the crossroads of mathematics, systems design, and art. My goal in life is simple: create experiences that move people, share them with the world, and keep pushing the boundary of what interactive art can be.

Outside of code I'm constantly in motion. I love to train, whether its lifting weights, rock climbing, or hiking whenever the opportunity comes, I have a deep hunger to experience as much of this planet as possible with all my senses. The same mindset that gets me up a difficult wall is what gets me through a difficult problem, show up, stay patient, stay consistent, find the way.

Forged Artifacts

⚙ Drive AI — Self-Driving Car
A self-driving car simulation built with Python and Pygame, using the NEAT algorithm — Neuro Evolution of Augmenting Topologies — to train neural networks through genetic evolution. Each car in the simulation is controlled by its own neural network that takes sensor inputs from the environment and outputs steering decisions, with the fittest networks surviving each generation and passing their traits forward. Over successive generations the cars learn to navigate increasingly complex tracks entirely on their own, with no hardcoded rules. The project also features a custom map builder, letting you design your own tracks and watch the neural networks adapt to new environments from scratch.
GitHub Live Demo
🗺 A* Pathfinding Algorithm
An interactive pathfinding visualiser built in Python and Pygame, implementing five algorithms — A*, Dijkstra's, BFS, DFS, Greedy Best-First, and Bidirectional BFS — so you can compare their behaviour side by side on the same layout. Each algorithm is visualised in real time, tracking nodes visited and path length, with adjustable animation speed and optional diagonal movement. Preset layouts include a randomly generated maze and a hand-crafted suburb street map designed to mimic real-world navigation. The project was built to develop a deeper understanding of graph search theory, heuristics, and the trade-offs between optimality and efficiency.
